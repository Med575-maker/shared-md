# High-Intensity Training (HIT) Program

## 1. Program Overview

This program follows a low-volume, high-intensity training approach inspired by Mike Mentzer's Heavy Duty philosophy.

The main goal is to maximize muscle growth while keeping training volume efficient and allowing enough time for recovery.

**Main principles:**
- Focus on quality working sets, not high volume.
- Take most working sets to 0–1 RIR.
- Use controlled movements and full range of motion.
- Prioritize progressive overload.
- Allow enough recovery between workouts.
- Avoid unnecessary sets that add fatigue without meaningful stimulation.

**Training philosophy:** Stimulate the muscle, recover, and grow.

---

## 2. The Science of Intensity & RIR

RIR means *Reps in Reserve*. It measures how many additional repetitions you could perform before reaching technical failure.

- **0 RIR:** Technical failure. You cannot complete another full-ROM repetition with proper form.
- **1 RIR:** You could perform one more repetition.
- **2 RIR:** You could perform two more repetitions.
- **3 RIR:** You could perform three more repetitions.

### Why train close to failure?

Muscle growth depends heavily on mechanical tension.

As a set becomes harder, more motor units are recruited to maintain force production. Training close to failure helps recruit and challenge high-threshold motor units.

This is why taking working sets close to failure is important for hypertrophy.

However, failure is not mandatory on every set. Training consistently at 0–2 RIR can produce excellent results.

**Important:** Technical failure is the goal, not sacrificing form to complete extra repetitions.

---

## 3. The Efficiency of Low-Volume / High-Intensity Training

The Heavy Duty philosophy, popularized by Mike Mentzer, focuses on getting the maximum possible stimulus from a small number of working sets.

Instead of performing 4–5 demanding sets for every exercise, this program uses 1–2 high-quality working sets.

### Why lower volume?

- The first hard sets provide a significant hypertrophy stimulus.
- Additional sets can provide diminishing returns.
- Excessive volume increases fatigue and recovery demands.
- More training does not automatically mean more muscle growth.

Once sufficient stimulus has been achieved, additional sets may contribute more fatigue than useful growth.

### Recovery matters

Muscles need time to repair and adapt after training.

High-intensity training can create substantial local and systemic fatigue. The nervous system also contributes to overall fatigue, particularly after demanding compound exercises.

However, CNS fatigue should not be blamed for every performance decline. Local muscle fatigue, sleep, nutrition and total training stress also matter.

The objective is to balance:
- Training stimulus.
- Muscle recovery.
- Systemic fatigue.
- Progressive overload.

**Remember:** Growth happens during recovery, not while performing endless sets in the gym.

## 4. Optimized Training Routine

### CHEST / BACK

- **Incline Machine Press** — 2 × 6–10 (0–1 RIR)
- **Machine Fly** — 1 × 10–15 (0 RIR)
- **T-Bar Row** — 2 × 8–12 (0–1 RIR)
- **Reverse Grip LP** — 2 × 6–10 (0–1 RIR)
- **Single Arm Row** — 1 × 8–12 (0–1 RIR)
- **Abs** — 2 × 10–20 (0–1 RIR)

### SHOULDERS / ARMS

- **Overhead Press (DB/Machine)** — 2 × 6–10 (1 RIR)
- **Dumbbell Lateral Raise** — 2 × 10–15 (0–1 RIR)
- **Rear Delt Exercise** — 1 × 10–15 (0 RIR)
- **Overhead Cable Extension** — 1 × 8–12 (0–1 RIR)
- **Hammer Curl** — 1 × 8–12 (0–1 RIR)
- **Cable Pressdown** — 1 × 10–15 (0 RIR)
- **Cable Curl** — 1 × 10–15 (0 RIR)

### LOWER 1

- **Squat** — 2 × 6–10 (1–2 RIR)
- **RDL** — 2 × 6–10 (1–2 RIR)
- **Hip Thrust** — 1 × 8–12 (0–1 RIR)
- **Leg Extension** — 1 × 10–15 (0 RIR)
- **Machine Calf Raise** — 2 × 8–15 (0–1 RIR)

### UPPER

- **Barbell Bench Press** — 2 × 6–10 (1 RIR)
- **T-Bar Row** — 2 × 8–12 (0–1 RIR)
- **Lat Pulldown** — 1 × 10–15 (0–1 RIR)
- **Dumbbell Skullcrusher** — 1 × 8–12 (0–1 RIR)
- **Incline Bicep Curl** — 1 × 8–12 (0–1 RIR)
- **Cable Lateral Raise** — 1 × 12–20 (0–1 RIR)

### LOWER 2

- **RDLs** — 2 × 6–10 (1–2 RIR)
- **Leg Extension** — 2 × 8–12 (0–1 RIR)
- **Leg Curl** — 2 × 10–15 (0–1 RIR)
- **Single Leg Calf Raise** — 2 × 10–15 (0–1 RIR)

## 5. Training Rules

### Working Sets
- Warm-up sets do not count as working sets.
- Perform 1–2 progressive warm-up sets before demanding exercises, adding more when necessary.
- Working sets should be controlled and challenging.
- Avoid unnecessary extra sets.

### Progressive Overload

Use double progression:

1. Start with a weight you can control within the prescribed rep range.
2. Gradually increase repetitions while maintaining the target RIR.
3. Once you reach the top of the rep range, increase the weight slightly.
4. Build repetitions again.

### Rest Between Sets

- Compound exercises: 2–4 minutes.
- Isolation exercises: 1.5–3 minutes.
- Take longer if necessary to maintain performance.

### Failure Guidelines

- Machine and isolation exercises: reaching 0 RIR is generally appropriate.
- Squats, RDLs and heavy presses: prioritize technique and safety. Stop around 1–2 RIR.
- Never sacrifice technique to force another repetition.

### Final Principle

Do not measure progress by how exhausted you feel after training.

Measure it by:
- Increasing strength.
- Increasing repetitions with the same weight.
- Maintaining proper technique.
- Recovering between sessions.
- Improving muscular development over time.

**Train hard. Keep volume productive. Recover properly. Progress consistently.**
